Every marketplace has the same first problem: buyers come for listings, and sellers come for buyers. Nobody wants to
be first. The good news is that you can solve it one side at a time, starting small and local, and most of it costs
nothing but your time.

## Start with listings, not visitors

A visitor who lands on an empty marketplace doesn't come back. Before you promote anything, make sure there's
something to see.

- **Post the first listings yourself**, or with friends and early partners. Real, good-quality listings with photos.
- **Import what you already have.** If you have a list of products, properties, jobs or members in a spreadsheet,
  [import it](/how-to-import-ads/).
- **Go to the sellers.** Contact the people who already sell what your site is about — on Facebook groups, in local
  newspapers, in shop windows — and offer to post their listings for free. Many marketplaces were built this way,
  one seller at a time.

Aim for at least a few dozen live listings in your main categories before you push for buyers. Sites with more
listings get far more visitors coming back.

## Pick a niche and a place

"Everything, everywhere" is the hardest marketplace to grow. "Used tractors in Ohio" or "rooms for students in
Leeds" is much easier: you know where your users are, what they search for, and who to talk to. You can always add
categories and places later.

## Free ways to get the word out

### Be where your users already are

Find the Facebook groups, subreddits, forums, WhatsApp and Telegram groups, and local noticeboards where your audience
buys and sells today. Be a useful member first: answer questions, give advice. Mention your site where it genuinely
helps, not in every post. Many groups have rules about promotion; read them.

### Local media

Local newspapers, radio stations, community magazines and news sites are always looking for local stories. "Local
resident launches free marketplace for farm equipment" is one. Write a short press release, send it to the editors,
and offer an interview. Check your own network too: a friend who works in local media can open doors.

### Partners

Clubs, associations, schools, shops and businesses connected to your niche can recommend your site to their members.
Offer them something in return: a free featured listing, a banner, or a page about them. Links from their websites
also help your [SEO](/seo-classifieds-website/).

### Offline

Flyers and posters in the places your users go (a farm supply store, a university noticeboard) still work,
especially for local marketplaces. Put your web address on them in large letters.

### Your own content

Write blog posts that answer the questions your users search for: buying guides, price guides, checklists. They bring
people in from Google and give you something to share. See [Blog](/how-to-create-a-blog/).

## Make sure it works on phones

Most visitors to a marketplace use a phone, and most sellers post from one, photos and all. Every current Yclas theme
adapts to any screen size, so there is nothing to switch on. Do check your own site on your phone after changing the
design, custom CSS or widgets, and post a test listing from your phone before you launch.

## Measure what works

Add [Google Analytics](/how-to-add-tracking-codes/) before you start promoting, and use a different link for each
channel (for example `?utm_source=facebook-group`) so you can see which ones bring visitors who post and reply.
Then do more of what works and drop the rest.
